Kenya KEPH Framework

Levels 1–6 explained

← Back

The Kenya Essential Package for Health (KEPH) organizes the health system into six service-delivery levels. SentinelHealth uses KEPH levels to scope which departments are available at each facility — a Level 2 clinic will only see outpatient departments, while a Level 5 referral hospital sees ICU and specialist units.

L1

Community Health Unit

Catchment: ~5,000 people (~1,000 households)

Services: preventive and promotive care delivered by Community Health Promoters (CHPs). Referral to L2/L3 as needed.

L2

Dispensary / Clinic

Catchment: ~10,000 people

Services: basic outpatient care, minor procedures, essential medicines. Staffed by nurses and clinical officers.

L3

Health Centre

Catchment: ~30,000 people

Services: inpatient maternity, basic surgery, laboratory, and pharmacy. Primary referral point from L2 facilities.

L4

Sub-County Hospital

Catchment: ~100,000 people

Services: inpatient and outpatient, surgery, specialized clinics, laboratory, radiology, and emergency care. Departments: ED, maternity, pediatrics, wards, laboratory, pharmacy.

L5

County Referral Hospital

Catchment: ~1,000,000 people

Services: advanced medical treatment, specialist care, referral centre for L4 facilities. Departments: ED, ICU, maternity, pediatrics, wards, laboratory, radiology, kitchen, laundry, waste management.

L6

National / Tertiary Hospital

Catchment: national

Services: highly specialized care and research. Examples: Kenyatta National Hospital, Moi Teaching and Referral Hospital, Kenyatta University Teaching, Referral and Research Hospital.

How SentinelHealth uses KEPH: When a Health Officer creates a shift roster, the department dropdown is filtered by the facility's KEPH level. A worker at a Level 2 clinic cannot select ICU, because the database rejects the combination. This prevents accidental data contamination and keeps dashboards meaningful.
